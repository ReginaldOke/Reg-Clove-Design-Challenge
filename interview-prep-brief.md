# Clove design challenge - prototype summary for interview prep

Context: I'm Reg, interviewing for Principal Product Designer at Clove (a cooking app: save recipes, plan meals, shop groceries, cook). The design challenge asked for ideas to improve the first-time user experience. I built a fully working interactive prototype (plain HTML/CSS/JS, no framework) and will walk Anna and Sam through it live. Live prototype: https://reginaldoke.github.io/Reg-Clove-Design-Challenge/

## The core thesis

New users hit Clove with empty state everywhere and onboarding forms nobody wants to fill in. My concept: **Clove's AI builds a memory of you from your behaviour, not from forms**, and **contextual nudges keep you moving so you never hit a dead end** in your first mile: save a recipe, plan a week, shop it, cook it, and get set up to do it all again. The prototype demonstrates one continuous end-to-end journey with AI memory accumulating visibly along the way.

## The flow, step by step

1. **Save from TikTok.** A new user's realistic entry point: a pixel-accurate TikTok player showing a Caesar salad recipe video. Share sheet, then the iOS share sheet, then the Clove import sheet. While the recipe saves (progress bar, spinning clove logo), two memories land as pills: "Memory: Finds recipes on TikTok" and "Memory: Quick, high-protein meals". Zero onboarding, and Clove has already learned two things from a single natural action.
2. **Explore the recipe.** The imported recipe with video hero, twists, live ingredients list. Changing the serving size from 6 to 4 scales every ingredient quantity live AND saves a memory ("Cooks for 4") with a toast that stays pinned until tapped. Key point: routine product actions double as memory capture.
3. **The memory card.** Tapping Edit on any memory toast opens My Kitchen, where "Clove's memory" is the first card: every memory with its provenance ("Household · noticed from recipe scaling", "Habit · noticed from this save"). Fresh ones carry a pink dot. Each row has a pencil that opens a purpose-built editor (steppers with illustrated plates, rulers, option lists), and rows swipe to delete with undo. Key point: the memory system is transparent and fully user-controllable, which is the trust story.
4. **Voice note: make it higher in iron.** Back on the recipe, the clove AI button morphs into a voice bar; "Make this higher in iron" types itself out. Under a soft AI glow, ingredients swap live (cos lettuce to baby spinach, croutons to toasted pepitas) with a "✦ Higher iron" tag, then the bar grows into a chat surface explaining the swaps in plain terms ("About 4mg more iron a serve"). Clove also adds a health goal, announced by a pinned green toast: "Health goal: More iron".
5. **The health tracker (redesigned after feedback, see below).** Tapping Edit on the goal toast opens Health goals: an illustrated "dressed plate" per goal (a drawn steak plate for iron) wearing its progress ring, swipeable between goals. Below: "Goal: Eat 5 iron-rich meals" with dinner thumbnails you tick off as cooked (the plate pulses and the ring eases forward), a dinner-driven week chart, and a pink contextual nudge: "Plan meals to hit your iron goal".
6. **Plan the week.** The nudge lands on an empty week and Clove asks exactly two questions in a card that grows out of the AI button: which days do you cook (day chips) and daily macro targets (steppers). Answers become two more memories ("Plans dinners 4 nights a week", "Aiming for 180g protein a day"), then the week builds meal by meal, iron-rich dinners staggering in under the glow, topped by a green "Grocery List · 6/6 recipes" tile.
7. **Groceries and the shop ask.** The grocery list (22 items, real product data) with a sticky "Start shopping" CTA. Tapping it asks two more questions: what do you value when shopping (low prices / quality / convenience) and a weekly budget set on a custom drag dial. Two more memories.
8. **Compare every way to buy.** Clove scans stores and delivery services ("Checking who has all 36 items…" with a full-page AI glow), then shows "36 items, 10 ways to buy": Delivery tab (Woolworths, Coles, Milkrun, Uber Eats with real prices, stock callouts like "Kale sold out") and a Nearby stores tab, because like 80% of Australians the user still shops in store. Nearby has store cards plus a live map (Leaflet) with price-labelled pins for 11 stores.
9. **Woolworths detail with swipe-to-swap.** Opening the store shows line items; a one-time hint animation reveals that cards swipe to substitute (rump steak to eye fillet etc., real Woolworths products and prices) with totals recounting live.
10. **Shop the list in store.** "Shop this list at Woolies" regroups the entire grocery list by aisle in store-walk order (Fresh produce · Front of store, Aisle 3, Aisle 9, Back wall) with product photos so you can find things on the shelf. This is the offline/in-store support piece.
11. **Check off and cook.** Ticking one item cascades the rest (you did the shop). No dead end: a nudge appears, "Cook tonight: Crispy Chicken Caesar Salad", leading back to the recipe with method steps ready.
12. **The leftover loop.** After cooking, the user tells Clove by voice: "I didn't use all the spinach". Confirm-first AI: Clove asks before changing anything ("Want me to update this recipe to use less spinach next time?"), one CTA at a time. Update recipe trims the quantity under the glow with a note ("you had some left over"); then Clove offers to add a spinach dinner to the plan. Update meal plan drops "Spinach and Ricotta Pasta · ✦ Uses leftover spinach" into Friday, chat confirming it. The first mile ends with the NEXT loop already started: plan, shop, cook again.

## The UX decisions and their rationale (the interview meat)

- **Memory from behaviour, not forms.** Every memory is earned by something the user did anyway (importing, scaling serves, answering an in-context question, a voice note). Each shows its provenance, so the AI never feels like surveillance; it feels like a good kitchen assistant paying attention.
- **Total memory transparency.** Every memory toast has Edit. The memory card shows everything, everything is editable with bespoke controls, deletable with undo. AI personalisation with a visible, controllable model of you.
- **Contextual nudges kill dead ends.** The pink AI card appears wherever Clove can help next (recipe → plan, tracker → plan, list done → cook, leftovers → plan). They are in-UI cards, not modals or floating pills: calm, dismissible, always relevant to the screen they sit in. A first-time user is never left staring at a finished screen wondering what's next.
- **Ask at the moment of relevance.** The planning questions appear when you ask to plan; the budget question appears when you start shopping. Two questions maximum per moment, always skippable, and the answers are stored as visible memories rather than buried settings.
- **Confirm-first AI, one CTA at a time.** The AI never rewrites anything from a voice note without asking, and never shows two competing actions at once. Trust through consent and pacing.
- **Progress in dinners, not milligrams.** The health tracker deliberately avoids diet-app framing: goals are "Eat 5 iron-rich meals", progress is dinners ticked off on an illustrated plate, and iron mg only appears as a supporting week chart. Clove is a cooking app; the unit of progress is a meal you cooked.
- **Respect the offline reality.** Delivery is one tab; in-store shopping is a first-class citizen (store comparison with stock and specials, the map, and the by-aisle list with product photos). Grocery shopping mostly happens in a physical store.
- **The loop closes.** The leftover flow exists so the demo ends where the product wins: waste avoided, the next week already forming, the habit loop engaged.
- **Craft.** All animation is deliberately buttery and minimal (soft springs, staggered reveals, a signature AI "glow" that frames whatever the AI is touching while it works). The AI has one consistent visual identity everywhere: the purple clove mark, the pink card, the glow, the sparkle memory disc.

## The feedback round (already responded to in the prototype)

Anna's notes on the original health tracker: (1) the primary goal (iron) wasn't legible among equal rings for protein and energy; (2) the dial felt functional but off-brand for Clove, "which isn't inherently a diet app", wanting a pinch more illustration.

Response: I built three concepts in a separate iteration deck ("dressed plate", "clean plate", "fill your plate") and shipped the dressed plate into the main flow. It answers both notes: one goal at a time is the whole screen (swipe to change), the graphic IS an illustration (a drawn plate of food wearing its ring), progress is dinners cooked, and provenance ("Added from your voice note") ties the goal back to the memory system. Good interview thread: showing feedback → ideation → shipped response within days.

## Extra colour if asked

- Everything is real and interactive: the serving stepper scales quantities with proper fractions, the budget dial has physics, the map is a real map, the Woolworths product data and prices are real, swipes have momentum and rubber-banding.
- The prototype has a presenter layer for the walkthrough: a controls panel listing every section with keyboard shortcuts (T TikTok, R Recipe, V Voice note, K Health tracker, P Plan, G Groceries, S Shop, L List, C Cook, H Home), arrow keys stepping the whole flow in order, and live step-by-step guidance where only the current action glows pink. Anyone opening the shared link lands on the TikTok step with this guide open, so the prototype can tour itself.
- Deliberate scope cuts: no real backend or auth, one recipe journey rather than breadth, hand-tuned data. The point was to make the end-to-end feel of the first mile tangible.
